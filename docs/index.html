How this is selected

  • Main page = registered trials only. Anything without a trial or published data appears only on the claims page.
  • No me-too drugs. A drug program is tracked only if it is first in class (a new mechanism or target) or aimed at aging itself. Devices are tracked as devices.
  • Programs are curated; trials are not. Each program carries a registry query and a match rule, and its trials are whatever the registry returns that pass the rule. Programs aimed at aging must also name aging in the trial's title, conditions or keywords (\b(aging|ageing|older adults?|elderly|frailty|senescen\w*|longevity|healthspan|geroscience|age-related)\b).
  • Non-drug interventions (exercise, fasting, mind-body, breathwork, heat and cold, sleep) must name aging, and only randomized trials count; exercise also needs at least 300 participants, because 1,357 exercise-and-aging trials are registered. Named programs, such as Joe Dispenza's retreat studies, are tracked by sponsor without those filters, and their design flags show what kind of evidence they are.
  • Trials are graded on the planned analysis, not the design. A brain implant or a retreat cannot be randomized, and a within-subject before/after comparison is a legitimate plan for a single-group study. Each trial scores 0–2 on up to five criteria, all read from the registry: prospective registration (registered before, or within 21 days of, the start; registering after primary completion scores 0); focused primary endpoint (1 = full marks, 2–3 = half, 4+ = none); analysis plan posted with the results (statistical analysis plan = 2, protocol only = 1; scored only once results are posted, since the registry expects them then); stated comparison (randomized, or a within-subject change from baseline; not scored when every primary endpoint is safety or feasibility, as in early device studies); and results reported (only where FDAAA requires results: phase 2+ drug trials and pivotal device trials, once a year has passed since actual primary completion). A criterion that does not apply is left out, not scored zero. Grade = share of available points: A ≥ 85%, B ≥ 65%, C ≥ 45%, D below. Hover a grade for its breakdown.
  • What to act on uses four fixed rules: results posted in the last 90 days; trials graded A or B reaching primary completion within 120 days; registry changes in the last 30 days and A/B-graded trials started in the last 120; and active trials graded D.
  • Example: Precision Neuroscience is not on the board because it has no registered trial yet.
